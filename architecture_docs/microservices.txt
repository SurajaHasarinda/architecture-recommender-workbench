Architecture: Microservices Architecture

Description: Microservices architecture is a design approach where an application is composed of small, independent services that communicate over well-defined APIs. Each service is self-contained, focuses on a specific business capability, and can be developed, deployed, and scaled independently. Services are loosely coupled and communicate through lightweight protocols, typically HTTP/REST or message queues.

KeyComponents: API Gateway, Service Discovery, Multiple Independent Services (User Service, Order Service, Payment Service, etc.), Service-Specific Databases, Message Queue/Event Bus, Load Balancer, Container Orchestration (Kubernetes), Monitoring and Logging System, Authentication Service, Configuration Management

Pros: Independent scalability of individual services. Technology diversity - each service can use different tech stacks. Faster deployment cycles through independent deployments. Better fault isolation - failure in one service doesn't crash entire system. Easier to understand and maintain individual services. Supports large distributed teams working independently. Enables continuous deployment and DevOps practices. Better resource utilization through targeted scaling. Easier to implement different data storage solutions per service.

Cons: Higher operational complexity requiring DevOps expertise. Network latency between services. Distributed system challenges (consistency, debugging, monitoring). Higher infrastructure costs initially. Complex deployment pipeline management. Difficult to implement transactions across services. Requires sophisticated monitoring and logging. Initial development slower than monolith. Increased memory consumption. More complex testing strategies needed.

UseCases: Large-scale applications with millions of users. Complex business domains that can be decomposed. Organizations with multiple autonomous development teams (10+ developers). Applications requiring independent scaling of different features. Systems needing high availability and fault tolerance. Projects using continuous deployment. Applications requiring technology diversity. E-commerce platforms with distinct domains. SaaS products serving enterprise clients. Systems with unpredictable or varying load patterns. Organizations with mature DevOps practices.
